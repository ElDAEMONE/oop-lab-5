QUESTION 2

a) Candidate nouns

Cable TV company, VTV, entertainment TV channels, person, customer,
customer details, package, registration, customer number, customer table,
package details, subscription, subscription table, month, bill, data,
channel details table, website, online tasks, list of channels, weekly TV
schedule, email, credit card, credit card details, amount, system,
payment details, payments table and week.

b) Selecting classes using the five rejection rules

1. Redundant nouns
   Person refers to Customer in this scenario, so keep Customer.
   Entertainment TV channel and channel refer to Channel.
   Package and subscription both describe the customer's selected channels
   here. Keep Subscription because no separate reusable package catalogue
   is described.
   Weekly TV schedule and TV schedule refer to TVSchedule.

2. Events or operations
   Registration becomes a register operation.
   Login, adding channels, removing channels, paying bills and sending
   emails are operations rather than separate entity classes.

3. Outside the scope
   The cable TV company, VTV and the physical credit card are outside the
   domain model. The model records payments but does not model a card issuer.
   Website and email transport belong to the interface/infrastructure.
   Month and week specify how often billing and notifications happen.

4. Meta-language
   System, data, details and online tasks are general descriptive terms.
   Customer table, subscription table, channel details table and payments
   table describe storage rather than additional domain classes.

5. Attributes
   Customer number and customer details belong to Customer.
   Payment amount belongs to Payment.
   Credit card details describe payment information rather than a separate
   class in this simple analysis.
   The list of channels requested for emails belongs to Subscription.

Selected classes:
Customer, Subscription, Channel, Bill, Payment and TVSchedule.

QUESTION 3

a) Candidate nouns

Online bookshop, things, books, magazines, CDs, products, information,
price, title, author, number of pages, artists, type, registered customers,
orders, ordered products, shipments, store, personal customers, payments,
delivery, business customers, invoice, customer types, credit card,
registration, contact information, personal information, authentication,
invoicing, bookshop system and reports.

b) Selecting classes using the five rejection rules

1. Redundant nouns
   Things and ordered products refer to Product.
   Registered customer refers to Customer.
   Store and online bookshop refer to the same business context.
   Keep Book, Magazine and CD as different product types because their
   specific information differs.
   Keep PersonalCustomer and BusinessCustomer because their payment and
   invoicing behaviour differs.

2. Events or operations
   Registration and authentication are customer-related operations.
   Delivery is represented by the shipment process.
   Invoicing is an operation that creates an Invoice.
   Adding/removing a customer, adding/viewing/updating a product,
   making/removing/viewing an order and generating/viewing reports are
   operations rather than extra entity classes.

3. Outside the scope
   The physical bookshop/store and physical credit card are outside this
   model. Payment records the method and transaction information.

4. Meta-language
   System, information, details and types are descriptive words, not
   independent domain classes.

5. Attributes
   Price and title are Product attributes.
   Author and number of pages can be Book attributes.
   Artist can be a CD attribute.
   Type can be stored as product-specific information.
   Contact and personal information belong to Customer.
   There is no requirement to manage authors or artists independently,
   so they are attributes in this model.

Selected classes:
Product, Book, Magazine, CD, Customer, PersonalCustomer,
BusinessCustomer, Order, Shipment, Payment, Invoice and ProductReport.

Main relationships:
Book, Magazine and CD extend Product.
PersonalCustomer and BusinessCustomer extend Customer.
A Customer can place several Orders.
An Order contains several Products.
An Order can have several Shipments.
A Shipment records which ordered products are included in that delivery.
An Invoice is associated with a business customer's order.
A Payment is associated with an order or its invoice.
ProductReport uses Product information to produce reports.

An OrderItem class can also be added during detailed design to keep the
quantity and price of each product in an order. It is derived from the
order-product relationship rather than being an explicit noun in the text.
